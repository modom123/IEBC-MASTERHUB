# T10 Teacher Guide: AI for Nonprofits

| | |
|---|---|
| **Format** | Self-paced video + monthly live Q&A; also offered as a sponsored live cohort |
| **Length** | 5 modules, about 4.5 hours of student time (about 70 minutes of video) |
| **Level** | Beginner |
| **Demo organization** | Northside Neighbors Alliance |
| **Case study** | Jacks of All Trades Community Development, using only what's published on the IEBC website |
| **Student deliverable** | Donor and volunteer workflow, a grant section draft and an impact dashboard |

## Teaching notes

- Students are often stretched thin: an executive director doing everything, or volunteers.
  Respect their time; every module gives a template they can use the same day.
- ★ **Compliance:** raffles, gaming and charitable solicitation have state rules. Donation
  receipts have IRS requirements. Say: *"Check your state's rules and your accountant before
  running a raffle or changing how you issue receipts."* Never present rules as settled in class.
- ★ **Grant integrity:** AI can help draft, but every fact, number and outcome must be true
  and verifiable. Some funders restrict or require disclosure of AI use; check each funder's guidelines.

## Course-level objectives

Students can organize donors and automate thank-yous, run online campaigns, draft grant
sections faster without losing accuracy, manage volunteers and program enrollment, and
report impact donors can see.

---

## Module 1: Donors and supporters (2 videos, ~14 min)

★ MUST TEACH: a donor CRM (even a well-built spreadsheet), giving history, thank-you within 48 hours (automated acknowledgment + personal note for larger gifts), and receipts issued by the giving platform or accounting system.

## Module 2: Fundraising campaigns (2 videos, ~14 min)

★ MUST TEACH: online giving pages, campaign emails written with AI from real stories (with consent from the people in them), event and raffle tools, and the compliance warning above.
◆ Case study: Jacks of All Trades' 50/50 raffle hub with ticketing (published features only).

## Module 3: Grant writing with AI (3 videos, ~20 min)

★ MUST TEACH:
1. Fit first: read the funder's priorities and eligibility before writing anything.
2. Build a "fact bank": mission, programs, numbers served, outcomes, budget, staff bios. AI only uses the fact bank.
3. Draft a needs statement and outcomes section with AI; edit for the funder's language.
4. ★ Mistake moment: AI invents a statistic ("72% of local youth…"). Delete it or replace it with a sourced number.
5. Check the funder's rules on AI use.

**Assignment 1:** Fact bank + one grant section drafted with AI and edited.

| Not yet | Meets | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| Unsourced statistics or generic language | Every fact traceable to the fact bank; matches funder priorities | Also a reusable outline for the next grant |

## Module 4: Volunteers and programs (2 videos, ~12 min)

★ MUST TEACH: volunteer sign-up and shift scheduling, reminders, program enrollment forms, and data care for minors and vulnerable people (collect only what you need, restrict access).

## Module 5: Impact reporting (2 videos, ~12 min)

★ MUST TEACH: pick 3–5 impact numbers, update them monthly, a one-page board report generated from the tracker, and a public impact page.
◆ Case study: Jacks of All Trades' live impact tracker (published features only).

**Assignment 2 (final):** Donor/volunteer workflow + one-page impact dashboard.

| Not yet | Meets | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| Missing thank-you step or no real numbers | Thank-you flow working; dashboard with 3+ real numbers | Also a board report template |

## Sponsored cohort option

When a funder or workforce program sponsors a group, run the modules as 5 weekly 90-minute
live sessions following the Playbook timeline, with the same assignments. Send the sponsor
a summary at the end: attendance, assignments completed, and survey results (no individual
grades without student permission).
