# IEBC Academy Teaching Playbook

**Why this exists:** IEBC classes will be taught by different people. A student who takes
"AI Admin Automation" from one teacher in March and a friend who takes it from another
teacher in June should get the same outcomes, the same deliverable and the same quality.
This playbook is how we make that happen while still letting each teacher be themselves.

---

## 1. The one rule

**Every class ends with something working in the student's business.**

Not "they understood it." Not "they saw a demo." A working automation, a live website, a
filled-in template, a drafted grant section. If a session ends and a student has nothing
they built, the session failed, no matter how good the lecture was.

---

## 2. The IEBC Build-Along Method

Every lesson, live or recorded, follows the same four-step arc. Teachers can change
examples and stories; they don't change the arc.

| Step | What happens | Share of time | Teacher's job |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. SHOW** | Teacher demonstrates the skill once, start to finish, on the demo business. | 15–20% | Narrate *why* each step matters, not just what to click. Keep it under 10 minutes. |
| **2. TRY** | Students repeat the same steps on the same demo data, together. | 20–25% | Go step by step. Wait for thumbs-up before moving on. Producer watches chat for anyone stuck. |
| **3. BUILD** | Students apply it to *their own* business. | 40–50% | Circulate (breakout rooms or screen-share check-ins). Unblock, don't take over. |
| **4. SHARE** | 2–3 students show what they built; group debrief. | 10–15% | Celebrate, name one improvement per share, connect to the next lesson. |

**Talk time limit:** the teacher talks no more than one-third of any live session.
If you notice you've been talking for 15 minutes straight, stop and give the room a task.

## 3. What's fixed vs. what you can make your own

Each lesson plan marks content with these labels:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ★ **MUST TEACH** | Fixed. Every teacher covers this, in this order, every time. These are what the outcomes and assignments depend on. |
| ◆ **RECOMMENDED** | Cover it if time allows. Skip only if the group is behind. |
| ○ **YOUR CHOICE** | Your own examples, stories, extra tips. This is where your experience shines. |

**Fixed across all teachers:** learning objectives, ★ points, the demo business and its
data, the assignment, the rubric, session timing (±10 minutes), the safety rules in
section 7, and the student-facing promises on the website (deliverable, course credit,
response times).

**Yours to choose:** stories, analogies, extra examples from your own clients (anonymized),
which student shares, humor, pacing within a segment, which tool you demo *if* the plan
says "any assistant" or "any automation tool".

If you think a ★ point is wrong or out of date, don't skip it. Teach it, then file a
**Guide Change Request** (section 11) so every teacher gets the fix.

## 5. Roles

| Role | Live cohorts | Team trainings | Self-paced courses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lead Teacher** | Runs SHOW and SHARE, owns the room | Same | Records the videos, runs monthly Q&A |
| **Producer / Co-instructor** | Admits students, watches chat, manages breakout rooms, posts links, keeps time, takes notes for the teacher log | Required for groups over 12 | Not needed |
| **Reviewer** | Scores assignments against the rubric, writes feedback within 3 business days | Optional | Scores submitted assignments |

One person can be Lead and Producer for groups of 12 or fewer. Above 12, always have a
Producer. Breakout rooms should be 4–6 students.

## 6. Before, during and after every session

### 6.1 Prep checklist (48 hours before)

- [ ] Re-read the lesson plan for this session, including "Watch for" notes.
- [ ] **Dry-run every demo** on the current version of each tool. AI tools change monthly;
      a button that moved breaks a live class. Note anything that changed in the
      Guide Change Request form.
- [ ] Record a **backup video** or screenshots of each demo, in case the tool or internet
      fails during class.
- [ ] Check the roster: names, business types, enrolled-as (individual / team / sponsor),
      and each student's "what do you want to get out of this" answer from the hub's
      Academy page. Note 2–3 businesses you can reference by name.
- [ ] Send the pre-class email (template in each guide) with the join link, what to bring
      and any free accounts students should create *before* class.
- [ ] Open, in browser tabs: slides, demo business data, every tool you'll demo, the
      assignment page, the feedback survey link.

### 6.2 Standard session timeline (90-minute live session)

| Time | Segment |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Welcome, agenda, one-question warm-up in chat |
| 0:05–0:10 | Win of the week: 1–2 students share progress from last assignment (skip in session 1) |
| 0:10–0:25 | SHOW |
| 0:25–0:40 | TRY |
| 0:40–0:45 | Break |
| 0:45–1:15 | BUILD (breakout rooms or quiet build time with check-ins) |
| 1:15–1:25 | SHARE |
| 1:25–1:30 | Assignment, preview of next session, feedback survey |

Shorter or longer sessions keep the same proportions. Half-day workshops (T14) repeat the
arc 2–3 times with a longer break.

### 6.3 After every session (same day)

- [ ] Post the recording, slides and assignment link to the class space within 24 hours.
- [ ] Fill in the **Teacher Log** (template in section 13): attendance, what ran long,
      what confused people, tech issues, students who need follow-up.
- [ ] Message any student who missed the session or got stuck, offering the recording
      and a 15-minute office-hours slot.
- [ ] Update the student's status in the hub if anything changed (for example, cancelled).

## 7. Safety and honesty rules (non-negotiable)

1. **No real personal data in AI tools on screen or in exercises.** Students use the demo
   data or redacted data. Teach the rule explicitly in every class's first session:
   *"Never paste customer names, phone numbers, Social Security numbers, account numbers,
   health information or passwords into an AI tool unless your company has approved that
   tool for that data."*
2. **Always show an AI mistake.** In every class, at least once, show AI output that is
   wrong or made up and how you caught it. Students must leave knowing that AI output is
   a draft until a human checks it.
3. **Not legal, tax or financial advice.** In T03, T04, T10 and T16, say the disclaimer
   out loud at the start and on the first slide. When a student asks a question that
   needs a lawyer or CPA, say so and move on. Don't guess.
4. **Don't overpromise results.** Don't quote "hours saved" numbers you can't back up.
   Teach students to measure their own before/after (every course includes this).
5. **No hard selling.** You may mention once, at the end of the course, that the course
   fee is credited toward an IEBC build within 90 days and that the student can book a
   free call. That's it. If a student asks for help beyond the class, hand them to the
   IEBC team; don't pitch during class.
6. **Accessibility.** Turn on live captions. Share slides before class. Describe what's on
   screen ("I'm clicking the blue Save button in the top right") for anyone who can't see
   it well. Offer an alternative to on-camera shares.
7. **Recording consent.** Say at the start that the session is recorded for enrolled
   students only. Students can keep cameras off. Never post recordings publicly.

## 8. Teaching AI specifically: what works

- **Teach the pattern, not the button.** Tools change. Teach the reusable pattern (for
  example, "trigger → action → check" or "role, context, task, format") and treat the
  specific tool as one example of the pattern. If a student uses a different tool, they
  should still be able to follow.
- **Use the student's own work by the second half of every session.** Motivation jumps
  when the example is their inbox, their invoice, their customer.
- **Name the fear.** Some students worry AI will replace them or that they're "not
  technical." Say early: *"If you can write an email and fill out a form, you can do
  everything in this class."* For staff in team trainings, frame AI as removing the parts
  of the job nobody likes.
- **Prompting is writing.** Students who struggle with prompts usually struggle with being
  specific. Coach them to explain the task the way they'd explain it to a new employee.
- **Mixed levels:** give fast finishers a "stretch" task (each lesson plan has one) and
  pair them with someone who's stuck. Never let the fastest student set the pace.
- **When a demo breaks:** say so calmly, switch to the backup recording, and turn it into
  a lesson: *"This is real life with AI tools. Here's how I'd troubleshoot."*
- **When you don't know:** *"Good question. I don't know yet. I'll find out and post the
  answer in the class space by tomorrow."* Then do it.

## 9. Assignments, feedback and completion

- Every assignment has a **rubric with 3 levels**: *Not yet*, *Meets*, *Strong*. The rubric
  is in each guide. Reviewers score against it, not against their personal taste.
- Feedback format, every time: **one thing that works, one thing to improve, one next
  step.** Keep it under 150 words. Return within 3 business days.
- **Completion:** a student completes a course when every assignment reaches *Meets* or
  better. They can resubmit as often as needed during the course plus 30 days.
- **Certificates of completion** go out for all paid courses. The *Certification* program
  (T17) has its own stricter standard.
- **Calibration:** once a month, all reviewers score the same 3 anonymized assignments
  separately, then compare. Differences of more than one level get discussed until the
  team agrees, and the rubric wording gets tightened if needed.

## 10. Teacher onboarding: how a new teacher gets cleared for a class

A teacher is cleared **per class**, not for the whole Academy.

| Step | What | Signed off by |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Read this playbook and the class guide. Complete every assignment in the class yourself as a student would. | Self |
| 2 | **Shadow** one full run of the class (live or recordings) and fill in a Teacher Log as if you were teaching. | Lead teacher for that class |
| 3 | **Co-teach** one run: you lead at least half the sessions with the lead teacher present. | Lead teacher |
| 4 | **Solo, observed**: you teach a full run; an experienced teacher observes at least one session and scores it with the Observation Checklist below. | Academy lead |
| 5 | Score 5 sample assignments; your scores must match the calibration key within one level. | Academy lead |

**Observation Checklist** (score each 1–3):

- Followed the Build-Along arc; talk time under one-third
- Covered every ★ point, in order
- Every student had something built by the end
- Showed an AI mistake and how to catch it
- Followed the safety rules (no real data, disclaimers where required)
- Handled a question or problem calmly and accurately
- Stayed within ±10 minutes of the timeline
- Students' feedback-survey average of 4 out of 5 or higher

## 11. Keeping the guides current (version control)

- The guides live in this folder in the IEBC-MASTERHUB repository. The newest timestamped
  file is the current version. Don't edit your own private copy; propose changes instead.
- **Guide Change Request:** after any session, note in the Teacher Log what should change
  (a tool moved a button, a ★ point confused people, a timing is unrealistic). The Academy
  lead reviews requests every two weeks and publishes a new timestamped version.
- **Quarterly tool review:** every quarter, one teacher re-runs every demo in every guide
  and updates the "Tool notes" section of each guide.
- When the website catalog changes (price, length, outcome), the class guide changes too.
  The website course list (`ACADEMY_COURSES` in the website's `index.html`) is the source
  of truth for what's promised to students.

```
• Bring: a laptop (not just a phone), and [class-specific list from the guide]

[Teacher name], IEBC Academy
```

### 13.4 Self-paced video recording standard

- Each lesson video is **6–12 minutes**; split longer topics into parts.
- Same arc: 1-minute intro (what you'll build and why), SHOW, then a clear on-screen
  "Your turn" card with the TRY and BUILD steps written out, then a 30-second recap.
- Record at 1080p, zoom the browser to 125% so text is readable, use the demo data,
  turn on a cursor highlight, and add captions before publishing.
- Every video ends by pointing to the worksheet or assignment for that lesson.
- Re-record any video when the tool's screens change enough to confuse a student.
