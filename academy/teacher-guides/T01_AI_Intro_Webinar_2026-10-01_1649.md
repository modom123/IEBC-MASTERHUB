# T01 Teacher Guide: AI for Small Business Owners in 60 Minutes

| | |
|---|---|
| **Format** | Free live webinar on Zoom, recorded for replay |
| **Length** | 60 minutes |
| **Group size** | Up to 100 (Producer required) |
| **Level** | Beginner, no experience assumed |
| **Demo business** | Lakeside Home Services |
| **Student deliverable** | Their personal "First Five" automation list + IEBC starter prompt pack |

## Purpose of this class

This is the front door of the Academy. Most attendees are curious but skeptical, and many
feel behind. Your job in 60 minutes: (1) make AI feel practical and safe, (2) give them one
real win during the webinar, and (3) send them away with a short list of what to automate
first. A good webinar makes people want the next class because it was useful, not because
it was a sales pitch.

## Learning objectives

By the end, attendees can:
1. Explain the difference between an AI assistant, an automation and an AI agent in one sentence each.
2. Use a simple 4-part prompt (role, context, task, format) to get a useful draft.
3. Name at least five tasks in their own business worth automating, ranked by impact.

## Materials and prep

- Slides (IEBC template), 12–15 slides max
- "First Five" worksheet (fillable PDF or Google Doc copy link)
- IEBC starter prompt pack (10 prompts: follow-up email, quote cover letter, review reply,
  social post, job description, meeting summary, FAQ answer, policy draft, invoice reminder,
  "explain this to me simply")
- Lakeside demo files: a sample customer email, a photo of a receipt, one paragraph about a completed job
- One AI assistant open and signed in (any mainstream assistant), plus a backup recording
- Producer: poll questions loaded, chat welcome message, links ready to paste

### 0:00–0:05 · Welcome

**Objective:** People feel welcome and start participating in the first two minutes.

- ★ Say who you are in two sentences, and that IEBC builds these systems for real businesses.
- ★ Recording notice and that cameras can stay off.
- ★ Chat warm-up: *"In the chat: what kind of business are you in, and what task eats most of your week?"*
  Producer reads a few out loud. You'll reference these later.
- ○ A quick personal story about the first time AI saved you real time.

### 0:05–0:15 · What AI is (and isn't)

**Objective:** Attendees can sort tools into three buckets.

★ MUST TEACH:
1. **AI assistant**: you ask, it drafts (writing, summarizing, brainstorming).
2. **Automation**: when X happens, do Y, every time, no AI required (form → email).
3. **AI agent**: an assistant that can take steps on its own within rules you set, with a human signing off.
4. **Where AI is reliable today:** drafting, summarizing, reformatting, categorizing.
   **Where it still needs a human:** facts, math it can't check, anything legal/tax/medical, anything sent to a customer without review.
5. The data rule (Playbook §7.1), said plainly.

◆ RECOMMENDED: Rough cost picture: most assistants have free tiers; paid plans cost about as much as a streaming subscription.

**Check:** Poll: *"A form that emails you when someone fills it out is: an assistant / an automation / an agent?"* (Answer: automation.)

### 0:15–0:35 · Three live demos (SHOW)

Run each demo in about 6 minutes. Narrate the *why*.

**Demo 1: Customer follow-up in 30 seconds** ★
1. Paste the Lakeside sample customer email (a homeowner asking about a water heater quote).
2. Prompt with the 4 parts, typed slowly so people can read:
   *"You are the office manager at a small plumbing company (role). A homeowner asked for a
   water-heater replacement quote; we can visit Thursday or Friday (context). Write a friendly
   reply offering both times and asking for the heater's age (task). Under 120 words, no
   jargon (format)."*
3. Read the result out loud. Edit one sentence by hand. ★ Point out: *"I still read it before sending."*

**Demo 2: Receipt photo to bookkeeping entry** ★
1. Upload the receipt photo. Ask the assistant to extract vendor, date, total and a suggested expense category as a table.
2. ★ **Show the mistake moment:** point out anything it got wrong or guessed (category is often a guess). If it gets everything right, ask it for the tax deduction amount and show that it shouldn't be trusted for that. Say: *"Draft, then check."*

**Demo 3: A week of social posts from one job** ◆
1. Paste the paragraph about a completed job. Ask for 5 short posts for different days, each with a different angle (before/after, tip, customer story, team, offer).
2. Show how to ask for changes: *"Make post 3 sound more like a person and less like an ad."*

**Watch for:** People asking which tool is "best." Answer: *"The one you'll use. Today's skill works in all of them."*

### 0:35–0:45 · Try it now (TRY)

**Objective:** Every attendee gets one real result during the webinar.

- ★ Producer pastes the 4-part prompt template into chat.
- ★ Attendees pick one real message they need to write this week (with private details removed) and use the template in any assistant.
- ★ Ask 2–3 people to paste their before/after into chat or unmute. Celebrate specifics.
- Stretch (for those already using AI): add *"Ask me 3 questions before you write"* to the prompt and compare results.

**Watch for:** Attendees without an AI account. Producer pastes signup links; they can follow along and try after.

### 0:45–0:55 · The time audit and your First Five

★ MUST TEACH:
1. Open the First Five worksheet. Explain the score: **how often × how long × how much you hate it** (each 1–3).
2. Live example: fill in three Lakeside tasks on screen (quotes, invoice reminders, appointment confirmations) and score them.
3. Attendees list and score their own tasks for 5 minutes. Producer plays quiet timer music.
4. Circle the top five. Explain: *"Your highest score is your first automation."*

### 0:55–1:00 · Q&A and next steps

- ★ Take 2–3 questions (Producer has picked them from chat).
- ★ Next steps, said once, without pressure:
  - Start with your #1 task this week using the prompt pack.
  - If you want to build automations with guidance: *AI Admin Automation* (self-paced) or the 4-week live cohort.
  - Industry tracks exist for trucking, trades, creatives and nonprofits.
- ★ Feedback survey link. Replay goes out within 24 hours.

## Common questions and suggested answers

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| "Is my data safe in these tools?" | Depends on the tool and plan. Default rule: don't paste personal or financial details unless your company has approved that tool for that data. Business plans often have stronger privacy terms. |
| "Will AI replace my staff?" | In small businesses it mostly removes the tasks nobody wants to do. The owners we work with use the time to take on more customers. |
| "Which tool should I buy?" | Start with a free tier of any mainstream assistant. Pay only when you hit limits on something you use every week. |
| "Can it do my taxes?" | It can organize records. Tax decisions belong with a licensed tax professional. |
