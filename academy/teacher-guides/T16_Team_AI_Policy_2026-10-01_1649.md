# T16 Teacher Guide: Responsible AI Policy and Safe-Use Training

| | |
|---|---|
| **Format** | 2 live sessions, 2 hours each, on video |
| **Session 1** | Policy workshop with leadership (3–8 people) |
| **Session 2** | Staff training (up to 50; Producer required) |
| **Client deliverable** | A ready-to-adopt AI use policy draft, an approved-tools list, and a trained team with sign-off |

★ **Disclaimer (say it and put it in the policy document):** *"IEBC provides a practical
policy template and training. This isn't legal advice. Have your attorney review the policy
before you adopt it, especially if you handle health, financial, or children's data, or
operate in a regulated industry."*

## Before session 1

- Intake questionnaire: industry, data types handled, tools staff already use (often more
  than leadership knows; suggest an anonymous staff survey), existing IT/security/privacy policies, regulators.
- Pre-fill the IEBC policy template with what you learned.

## Session 1: Policy workshop (2 hours)

**Objective:** Leadership makes the decisions the policy needs.

| Time | Segment |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | Why a policy: what's already happening (survey results), risks and benefits |
| 0:15–0:45 | ★ Decision 1: approved tools and accounts (business accounts only? which plans?) |
| 0:45–1:15 | ★ Decision 2: data categories: green (OK), yellow (approved tools only), red (never), with the company's real data examples |
| 1:15–1:20 | Break |
| 1:20–1:45 | ★ Decision 3: review rules: what must a human check before it goes to a customer, is published, or is used in a decision about a person |
| 1:45–2:00 | ★ Decision 4: ownership: who approves new tools, who answers questions, how incidents are reported; review date |

**Facilitation notes:** record decisions live in the template on screen. Where leadership
can't decide, write the open question and an owner. Don't decide for them.

**Policy template sections:** purpose and scope; approved tools; data categories with
examples; required human review; disclosure (when to tell customers AI was used); intellectual
property and confidentiality; incident reporting; training requirement; owner and review date.

**After session 1:** deliver the policy draft within 3 business days; client's attorney reviews before session 2 if possible.

## Session 2: Staff training (2 hours)

**Objective:** Staff can apply the policy to real situations.

| Time | Segment |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:10 | Leadership opens: why this matters, and that the goal is safe use, not no use |
| 0:10–0:35 | The policy in plain language: approved tools, green/yellow/red data, review rules |
| 0:35–1:05 | ★ Real AI mistakes: made-up facts, wrong numbers, confident errors, privacy leaks; how to check |
| 1:05–1:10 | Break |
| 1:10–1:40 | ★ Scenario practice: 8 scenarios from the company's real work; small groups decide "OK / OK with review / not OK," then discuss |
| 1:40–1:50 | Quiz (10 questions, 80% to pass, retakes allowed) |
| 1:50–2:00 | Acknowledgment sign-off; where to ask questions; survey |

## Deliverables checklist

- [ ] Policy draft (with disclaimer and attorney-review note)
- [ ] Approved-tools list
- [ ] Staff training recording + slides
- [ ] Quiz results and acknowledgment list for HR
- [ ] 6-month review reminder for the policy owner
