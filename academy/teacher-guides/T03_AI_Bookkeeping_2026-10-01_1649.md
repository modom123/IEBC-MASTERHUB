# T03 Teacher Guide: AI Bookkeeping: Receipts to Tax Export

| | |
|---|---|
| **Format** | Self-paced video course + monthly live Q&A and assignment review |
| **Length** | 5 modules, about 3.5 hours of student time (about 55 minutes of video) |
| **Level** | Beginner |
| **Demo business** | Lakeside Home Services (sample year of receipts, mileage and 2 contractors) |
| **Tools** | Efficient by IEBC (primary demo); principles apply to any bookkeeping tool |
| **Student deliverable** | A working bookkeeping setup with a tax-ready export for the current quarter |

## Teacher qualifications for this class

Teachers should be comfortable with small-business bookkeeping (categories, Schedule C,
1099 basics). If you aren't, co-teach with someone who is. **You are teaching a workflow,
not giving tax advice.**

★ **Disclaimer, said at the start of Module 1 and shown on the first slide of every module:**
*"This class teaches how to keep organized records. It's not tax or legal advice. For decisions
about your taxes, talk with a licensed tax professional."*

## Course-level objectives

Students can:
1. Separate business and personal money and set up categories that map to their tax form.
2. Capture receipts with AI scanning and correct mistakes in under a minute each.
3. Track mileage, recurring expenses and contractor payments.
4. Produce a clean quarterly export for their accountant or tax software.

---

## Module 1: Bookkeeping basics for owners (2 videos, ~12 min)

**Objective:** Students understand the minimum records they need and set up categories.

★ MUST TEACH:
1. The disclaimer (above).
2. Income, expenses, and why categories matter (they become lines on a tax form).
3. **Separate accounts.** One business bank account and card. Mixing is the #1 cause of bookkeeping pain.
4. What the accountant needs from you at year-end: categorized transactions, receipts, mileage log, contractor payments, and a profit and loss statement.

**SHOW:** Set up Lakeside's categories in Efficient, matched to Schedule C lines (advertising, car and truck, contract labor, supplies, etc.).
**BUILD:** Students set up their categories; if they already have an accountant, they ask which categories the accountant prefers.
**Watch for:** Students creating 40 categories. Coach: 12–20 is plenty for most small businesses.

## Module 2: AI receipt and invoice scanning (3 videos, ~15 min)

**Objective:** Students build a weekly 10-minute receipt habit.

★ MUST TEACH:
1. Snap → AI reads → review → file. The review step is not optional.
2. ★ Mistake moment: scan a faded receipt where AI reads $18.99 as $189.90, and a hardware-store receipt it files as "Office supplies" instead of "Supplies/materials". Show how to fix both.
3. What to check every time: date, total, vendor, category, business vs. personal.
4. Keep the image: the receipt image is the record, not just the number.
5. The weekly habit: same day, same time, 10 minutes.

**SHOW:** Scan 6 Lakeside receipts, fix 2 errors.
**TRY:** Students scan the 6 demo receipts (provided as images) and compare to the answer key.
**BUILD:** Students scan one real month.

**Assignment 1:** One month of receipts scanned and categorized + the demo answer-key check.

| Not yet | Meets | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| Demo receipts have 2+ uncorrected errors | Demo receipts match the key; one real month filed | Also set up auto-forwarding of email receipts |

## Module 3: Mileage, recurring expenses and budgets (2 videos, ~10 min)

★ MUST TEACH:
1. Mileage log essentials: date, start/end or miles, business purpose. GPS logging in Efficient.
2. Standard mileage rate vs. actual expenses: *mention both exist and that the choice is a tax decision for their tax professional.*
3. Recurring expenses (software, rent, phone) set up once.
4. Budget vs. actual: one glance a month.

**SHOW:** Lakeside's truck mileage for one week; recurring software subscriptions; a month of budget vs. actual.
**BUILD:** Students turn on mileage tracking and add their recurring expenses.

## Module 4: Contractors and 1099s (2 videos, ~8 min)

★ MUST TEACH:
1. Collect a W-9 **before** the first payment.
2. Track every contractor payment against the contractor.
3. Efficient flags contractors who may need a 1099. ★ Say: *"Whether a 1099 is required depends on the rules for that year and how you paid. Confirm with your tax professional."*
4. Employee vs. contractor is a legal question; don't answer it in class.

**SHOW:** Lakeside's two contractors: one paid by check (tracked), one paid by card (show where the flag differs and say to confirm with a tax professional).
**BUILD:** Students add their contractors.

## Module 5: Tax time in one click (2 videos, ~10 min)

**Objective:** Students produce a quarterly export.

★ MUST TEACH:
1. Quarterly estimate in Efficient is an estimate; their tax professional sets the real payment.
2. Export to QuickBooks or Schedule C format; what each is for.
3. Year-end checklist: all accounts reconciled, receipts attached, mileage complete, contractors reviewed.

**SHOW:** Export Lakeside's Q3.
**BUILD:** Students export their current quarter.

**Assignment 2 (final):** Quarterly export + completed year-end checklist (what's done, what's missing).

| Not yet | Meets | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| Export has uncategorized items | Export complete, checklist filled in | Also shows reconciliation to the bank statement |

## Monthly live Q&A (60 min)

Same structure as T02: fix-it clinic, mini-lesson on the month's common mistakes, open
questions. **Before answering any tax question, repeat the disclaimer.** Keep a list of
"questions for your tax professional" on screen and add to it when those questions come up.
Students like leaving with that list.

## Common questions

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| "Can I deduct X?" | That's a tax question; add it to your list for your tax professional. What I can show you is how to record it so they have what they need. |
| "I mixed personal and business for years." | Start clean from today with a separate account. Ask your accountant how to handle the past. |
| "Do I need to keep paper receipts?" | Keep the images; ask your tax professional about paper for your situation. |
